## Programme

- Problem hypothesis — Business lines maintain separate onboarding content; regional teams cannot easily distinguish common requirements from local changes. Completion reports hide unresolved capability gaps. Validate through interviews before investment.

- Programme mandate — Align Educational Design & Operations, three simulated business lines and regional organisations around a common service education lifecycle. Preserve local accountability and expert review.

- Scope — MR onboarding, software-workflow learning and remote-support education; EN/DE/TR delivery; common outcomes, journey, process, release gate, capability dashboard and a proposed 12-week rollout.

- Boundaries — No patient data, scanner operation, clinical decisions or product claims. Language is independent of region. This case does not demonstrate past employment in a global matrix organisation.


## Strategy

- Workforce readiness — Role-specific pathways and observed task evidence — Independent task success; observation coverage

- Customer success — Agree learning outcomes at handover and review gaps after onboarding — Time to agreed learning milestone; unresolved questions

- Operational consistency — Common source register, release gates and local exceptions — Current-version coverage; rework per release

- Investment discipline — Pilot before scale; prioritise observable performance over activity counts — Cost per reassessed learner; conservative benefit scenarios


## Customer journey

- 01 · Discover — Customer training lead + service educator — Interview and role/task inventory — Assumption: generic requests hide different needs — Approved learning brief with task and baseline

- 02 · Prepare — Regional coordinator — Role-specific invitation and prerequisite check — Assumption: responsibilities are unclear — Correct pathway selection and prerequisite completion

- 03 · Learn — Learning designer + content owner — Digital module, live demonstration, reflective task — Assumption: content overload increases uncertainty — Task accuracy and requests for assistance

- 04 · Apply and escalate — Learner + designated expert — Decision simulation and contextual handoff — Assumption: completion is mistaken for readiness — Appropriate escalation and complete context

- 05 · Verify — Authorised assessor — Novel task and observation rubric — Assumption: quiz scores hide transfer gaps — Observed task success; missing evidence remains visible

- 06 · Sustain — Customer success lead + education owner — Targeted refresher and 30-day review — Assumption: repeated full courses waste effort — Reassessment coverage, gap closure and customer feedback


## Operating model

- Global education owner — Owns portfolio outcomes, priorities and release accountability. Resolves cross-business-line conflicts in a monthly steering review.

- Business-line subject experts — Own technical correctness and version applicability. A business priority cannot override required expert review.

- Regional organisations — Own local workflow fit, language review, facilitator preparation and support contacts. Exceptions require documented impact and approval.

- Design & Operations — Designs modular content, accessibility checks and assessment. Operations manages assignments, completion tracking and release records.

## Learning simulation

Workflow learning only; no clinical instruction. Decisions are stored in this tab until refresh. Switching language preserves them. No data is transmitted.

### MR onboarding

The course is complete, but the learning record has an unanswered question about the applicable resource. What should happen?

A. Close the question because the course is complete

B. Keep it pending and send contextual information to the designated expert

C. Remove the question from the report

Appropriate decision: B

Completion is not evidence that uncertainty has been resolved. Preserve the gap and assign an accountable reviewer.

### Software workflow

A learner opens a guide for version 2 while the fictional training environment uses version 3. No approved mapping is available.

A. Use the older guide without checking

B. Generate missing instructions automatically

C. Pause the affected activity and request a version-matched approved source

Appropriate decision: C

Source applicability is a release dependency. AI or learner confidence cannot substitute for version review.

### Remote-support education

An educator receives a recurring learning question without the module ID, version or previous steps. What improves the handoff?

A. Capture module, version and attempted steps; route to the designated role

B. Mark the learner as competent

C. Forward an empty ticket to every department

Appropriate decision: A

A structured service handoff reduces clarification loops. It remains an educational query, not a clinical escalation system.


## Measurement

Synthetic cohorts only. Success is successful / observed, not successful / completed. Gap closure is closed / reassessed; show reassessment coverage alongside it. No before/after causal effect is claimed.

Proposed pilot: 8–12 consenting adult volunteers complete fictional learning tasks. Record assistance, first-attempt accuracy, task time and qualitative confusion. Test a revised version with participant overlap disclosed. At 30 days use a new task if feasible. Targets: ≥80% unassisted task success and no unresolved critical usability defect. Targets are not achieved results; convenience sampling and practice effects limit inference.

Illustrative assumptions, not realised savings. Potential released time is not cash savings. Adoption is assumed to be 100%; test a lower effective learner count for sensitivity. One-time build cost and annual support are included.


## Delivery & agile

- Weeks 1–2 — Discover & align — Interview guide, capability baseline, stakeholder map and signed scope. Gate: sponsor agrees outcomes and pilot audience.

- Weeks 3–4 — Design common core — Journey, RACI, source register, operating model and first scenario. Gate: expert review dependency identified.

- Weeks 5–6 — Prototype & localise — EN/DE/TR parity, facilitator guide and version controls. Gate: functional and language review.

- Weeks 7–8 — Pilot & evaluate — Usability sessions, observed task evidence, defect log and revised content. Gate: evidence supports limited rollout.

- Weeks 9–12 — Roll out & improve — Train facilitators, release by region, monitor support and review outcomes. Gate: named service owner accepts handover.

Use a prioritised backlog and a weekly inspect-and-adapt cycle. Limit work in progress to two items. Ready means a clear user need, acceptance criteria and dependencies. Done means an implemented artefact, passing checks, evidence and a release note. A board alone is not Scrum; no Scrum Master certification or historic agile delivery is claimed.

Weekly review: demonstrate a task → compare acceptance criteria → log feedback → choose the next priority. Monthly steering: scope, dependencies, investment and exception decisions. Regional facilitator handover: objectives, demo, rubric, escalation route and version changes.
